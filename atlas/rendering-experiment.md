# Comparing the two map collections

## What this edition ships

The showcase contains two equal collections. **Paper studio** has eight individual forms; **Origami voyages** has eight scenes with opened charts, moving paper bodies and small origami boats. Both expose exactly the same nine discoveries, explanations, evidence labels and subject destinations.

New visitors begin at the shared workbench with Origami voyages moving in its live preview. Readers can switch collections directly; the corresponding body stays selected, making a comparison easier. Their chosen collection and last form are stored in their own browser to continue the visual sequence. There is no random traffic assignment, experiment-event transmission or automated promotion in this edition.

Equal catalogue size is distinct from a 50/50 traffic experiment. The latter should start only after both collections pass the same interaction and rendering checks.

## A concrete first experiment

Randomly assign eligible visits 50/50 to a versioned studio entrance or voyage entrance, keeping the assignment consistent for a returning browser during the experiment. Offer the same visible switch so readers retain control. Analyse the originally assigned group separately from readers’ later switches.

| Measure | Definition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject-entry rate | Visits that open a subject explanation divided by eligible map visits | The map should lead into the research. |
| Explanation-to-detail rate | Readers who follow the primary subject link after selecting a discovery | Distinguishes decorative engagement from the intended reading journey. |
| Time to first subject | Elapsed time from a usable entrance to the first subject opening | Helps identify a beautiful but confusing entrance. |
| Rendering failure rate | Visits that enter the readable fallback divided by eligible visits | A successful design must remain usable. |
| Responsiveness | Frame-time and interaction-delay summaries by broad viewport/device class | Keeps heavy scenes from winning through appearance while making navigation worse. |
| Collection switch rate | Assigned visits that explicitly select the other collection | Records expressed preference without treating it as conclusive alone. |

Keep the event specification small: version, assigned collection, selected collection, action type, elapsed interval and broad rendering mode. A future analytics implementation needs a chosen storage destination and an explicit retention policy; neither is configured here. Do not send research reading histories or personal identifiers merely to compare the art direction.

Predefine the observation window, minimum useful improvement and comparison method before seeing the results. Report denominators and uncertainty. Do not treat a handful of extra clicks or repeated checks by the authors as a winner.

## A later adaptive experiment

A contextual bandit could adjust entrance selection after a reliable baseline experiment. That would require a stable reward definition and enough genuine visits to evaluate it. Maximise useful entry into the research subject to rendering and accessibility constraints; raw time on page or motion-control clicks alone are poor substitutes for that outcome.

Keep an exploration share and a fixed comparison group so changes remain assessable. Version the scenes and measurement rules. Human review should decide when an observed improvement is ready to become the default. The system should never change mathematical evidence labels, citations or private/public boundaries as part of a visual experiment.

No bandit or reinforcement-learning claim is made for the current website. This document defines a reviewable next experiment; it does not report visitor data or a measured winner.
