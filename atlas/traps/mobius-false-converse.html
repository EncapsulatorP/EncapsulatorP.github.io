REJECTED CLAIM

Does the Möbius sign certify twins?

This sentence is false. Its precise correction remains available below.

If μ(m² − 1) = +1 and μ(m²) = 0, then m − 1 and m + 1 must both be prime.

The correction

The implication from twin primes to this sign pattern is true. Its converse is false. The exact counterexample at m = 34 remains a mathematical treasure.

Set m = 34. Then m² − 1 = 1155 = 3 × 5 × 7 × 11 is squarefree with four prime factors, so μ(1155) = +1. The next integer is 34², so μ(1156) = 0.

Nevertheless, m − 1 = 33 = 3 × 11 and m + 1 = 35 = 5 × 7 are both composite. Thus the required sign pattern holds while the claimed conclusion fails.

For integers m ≥ 3, the stronger condition Ω(m² − 1) = 2 is equivalent to both neighbours being prime: Ω is additive on the product (m − 1)(m + 1), and both factors are at least two.

m = 34: (μ(1155), μ(1156)) = (+1, 0)
33 = 3 × 11 and 35 = 5 × 7 are composite
For m ≥ 3: Ω(m² − 1) = 2 ⇔ both neighbours are prime

What remains true

  • Twin primes do imply the adjacent Möbius pattern (+1, 0).
  • The m = 34 counterexample is an exact refutation of the converse.
  • The Ω = 2 characterization retains its stated domain m ≥ 3.
