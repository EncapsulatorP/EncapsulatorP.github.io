REJECTED CLAIM

Do two bases remain at the summit?

This sentence is false. Its precise correction remains available below.

Every upper height from just above one through the maximum, including the maximum itself, is attained by two distinct finite real bases.

Read the correction

The correction

Two distinct bases exist only strictly below the maximum. At the maximum height the inverse branches meet, leaving exactly one finite real base.

For u = c^(−v), v = c^u and c > 1, set t = v log c. The exact identity v log v = t exp(−t) reduces the question to a single curve.

Its derivative is exp(−t)(1 − t). The unique maximum 1/e occurs at t = 1, so the maximum height v_max = exp(W₀(1/e)) has only that one value of t.

Recovering c = exp(t/v) gives the unique base c_peak = exp(1/v_max). Interior heights 1 < v < v_max have two bases; heights above the maximum and height one have none among finite c > 1.

v log v = t e⁻ᵗ ≤ 1/e
v_max ≈ 1.3210997620; c_peak ≈ 2.1317539817
At the maximum: t = 1, so exactly one finite base

What remains true

  • The full convergence result remains valid for every real c > 1.
  • Every strict interior height has exactly two distinct finite bases.
  • The maximum height is attained; c_peak is not a largest permitted base.
