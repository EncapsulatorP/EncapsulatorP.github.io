A square next door to every twin-prime product

Twin primes supply an adjacent (+1, 0) pattern in the Möbius function. Counting factors with multiplicity gives the exact two-way condition.

Hibernating Lemmas · recovered prime-pair conversation

μ(p(p + 2)) = +1,  μ((p + 1)²) = 0
For m ≥ 3:
Ω(m² − 1) = 2 ⇔ m − 1 and m + 1 are prime

Why follow it

It preserves the original adjacent-pattern observation and identifies the additional information needed for an exact characterization.

Distinct primes give a squarefree product with two prime factors; the next number is (p + 1)². This proves the displayed Möbius implication.

For m ≥ 3, both factors in (m − 1)(m + 1) are at least 2. Their Ω values add, so a total of two forces each factor to be prime.

The Möbius pattern alone also occurs at m = 34: 1155 = 3·5·7·11, followed by 34². The existing Disappointment Islands entry keeps this useful counterexample alongside the original identity.
