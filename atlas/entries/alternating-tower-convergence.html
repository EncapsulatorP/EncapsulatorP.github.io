Two alternating phases, one convergent construction

A real sign-flipping tower has a unique lower and upper phase for every base c > 1. The construction admits a uniform contraction argument.

June–September 2026 tower work; proof recovered and checked for this edition

u = c^(−v),  v = c^u
F_L(v) = exp(L exp(−Lv)),  L = log c
|F′_L(v)| ≤ 4 exp(1/e − 2) < 1  (v ≥ 1)
Key formula

u = c^(−v) · v = c^u

Why follow it

It turns the recurring two-branch pictures into a precise object with existence, uniqueness and an explicit convergence bound.

Every starting value v₀ ≥ 1 enters [1, exp(1/e)] after one update. The derivative bound q ≈ 0.7820581366 then gives a contraction on that interval. Recover the lower phase with u = exp(−Lv).

At c = 1.11, the recomputed phases are u ≈ 0.89177745064481416237 and v ≈ 1.09753406961538895124. They belong to the imposed alternating-sign system.

The earlier public solver implemented the bound, while its linked proof note was absent from the default branch. This edition supplies the written proof. The decimal calculations report residuals; they are not outward-rounded interval certificates.
