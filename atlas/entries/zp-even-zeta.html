Z(p): a Bernoulli reduction worth keeping

The prime-indexed exponential family has an exact even-zeta reduction to rational Bernoulli coefficients.

Z(p) conversations · November 2025 onward

Z(p) = exp(πζ(p−1)/p) + 1 = exp(c_p π^p) + 1
c_p = (−1)^((p+1)/2) 2^(p−2) B_(p−1)/(p(p−1)!)
c₃=1/18,  c₅=1/450,  c₇=1/6615

Why follow it

The family and its test harness survive as a precise construction, with a clean separation between algebraic reduction and harder arithmetic questions.

The formula applies to odd primes p, so p−1 is a positive even integer. It follows from the classical even-zeta formula. p = 2 is excluded because ζ(1) has a pole.

Rational c_p and transcendental π do not by themselves determine the arithmetic nature of exp(c_p π^p)+1. That question needs a separate argument.

Bounded integer-relation searches can still be useful when precision, degree, coefficient bounds and the searched quantity are all recorded.
