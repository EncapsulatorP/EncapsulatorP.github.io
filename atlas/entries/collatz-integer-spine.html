A fixed integer spine in a changing complex map

An entire extension agrees with the standard Collatz rule on integers. Sine perturbations can change the complex landscape while preserving that agreement.

Collatzscape and showcase metamorphoses · February–November 2026

C(z) = [2 + 7z − (2 + 5z) cos(πz)]/4
C(2k) = k,  C(2k+1) = 3(2k+1)+1
C_a(z) = C(z) + a sin(πz)/4

Why follow it

It gives the visual mutations a precise anchor and lets us ask which observed features depend on the chosen extension.

Substitute cos(πn) = (−1)^n to obtain the even and odd integer branches. Since sin(πn) = 0, the displayed perturbation leaves every integer value unchanged.

Many entire extensions share the same integer dynamics and differ elsewhere. Finite escape-time plots should therefore identify the extension, parameters and iteration limit.

The existing Collatz Metamorphoses exhibit explores this family; its geometric names describe views of the model.
