A phase inside the Sophomore’s dream

A complex phase parameter enters an integral whose power series is available exactly.

June conversation recovered in The Pirate Chest

∫₀¹ u^(−a u) du = Σ_{n≥1} a^(n−1)/n^n
For the phase study: a = exp(2πiα(x²−y+1))

Why follow it

Phase returns can be understood through an exact series before the geometric pattern is interpreted.

Use u^(−au)=exp(−a u log u) for real 0<u≤1, with its continuous endpoint limit. Since |u log u|≤1/e, the exponential series can be integrated term by term for every fixed finite complex a.

The substitution u=exp(−t) gives ∫₀¹(−u log u)^k du = k!/(k+1)^(k+1). Dividing by k! and reindexing produces the displayed series.

At phase returns α(x²−y+1)∈ℤ, a=1 and the integral takes the familiar value approximately 1.291286. The identity defines what the phase picture is measuring.
