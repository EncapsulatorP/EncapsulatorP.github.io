Why follow it

A shared measurement pipeline makes a visual spectral pattern interpretable rather than dependent on a peak detector’s settings.

The public runner compares a real-product variant, a leading Riemann–Siegel approximation and full Hardy Z(t), with common sign-change scans and controls.

Minimum peak spacing can impose apparent repulsion. Mean division alone is not a general unfolding procedure. The observable and its normalization need to be fixed before comparing spacing laws.

No new Poisson-to-GUE result is claimed by this atlas edition: the source protocol is recovered, while a complete new result table has not been rerun here.
