A zeta landscape with a movable branch cut

A branch-controlled zeta transform feeds a finite geometric sum, producing a precise experimental dynamical map.

XO₂ and zeta-branching conversations · recovered 2026 implementation

w = ζ((z−a)^α + b),  r = λ/(1+exp(w))
K(z) = Σ_{j=1}^N r^j
= r(1−r^N)/(1−r),  with K=N when r=1

Why follow it

The branch convention becomes a visible control rather than an invisible change in the object being plotted.

The source’s XO₂ defaults include a = 0, b = 1.25, α = 1/2 and N = 3. The complex power requires a chosen logarithm branch; poles and overflow must remain separate from finite values.

The finite geometric-sum formula is exact. Iteration of K is the experimental model, and its finite escape or survival images do not inherit theorems about zeta zeros.

Direct rendering and learned surrogates are different computational paths; compare them against the same branch convention and sampled inputs.
