Reverse two prime layers; change the second harmonic

Two prime-indexed maps share a fixed point and composition slope, while the order of the layers appears in the quadratic response.

Ω-wave and Prime Forms threads · 20–21 November 2026

Ω = W(1),  T_p(x) = Ω p^(Ω−x)
F_pq = T_q ∘ T_p
½(F_pq−F_qp)(Ω+u) = (Ω²/4)log p log q log(q/p)u² + O(u³)
Key formula

Order changes the u² term; the slope is shared.

Why follow it

The signed wave artwork has a measurable analytic effect behind it: order enters one derivative beyond the common linear response.

Both compositions fix Ω and have derivative Ω² log p log q at that point. Expanding one order and subtracting the other gives the displayed coefficient; reversing p and q reverses its sign.

For u = ε sin θ, the quadratic term contains a constant and a second harmonic. Subtracting its mean leaves the second harmonic; halving ε reduces its leading amplitude by a factor of four.

The statement is a local expansion for the defined maps. The Prime Forms source preserves Neo’s visual layout and helper work, alongside the earlier Ω analysis and the user’s initiating questions.
