Prime paths on a Fermat-shaped surface

Arithmetic sampling paths can be projected exactly onto an absolute-value power surface, then compared by coverage and spacing.

Recovered boundary-path notebook and implementation

r = (|d_x|^n + |d_y|^n + |d_z|^n)^(−1/n)
x = r d ⇒ |x₁|^n + |x₂|^n + |x₃|^n = 1

Why follow it

The visual question is concrete: which path fills a chosen surface most evenly at a fixed sampling budget?

For a nonzero direction d and n > 0, substitution verifies the radial projection exactly. The source supplies Fibonacci, Hilbert, spiral, radical-derived and prime-gap paths.

The absolute-value surface is the defined real geometric object. Separate Mandelbulb renders use finite escape-time approximations and have their own numerical settings.

A compelling image becomes a measurable experiment when coverage, nearest-neighbor spacing and total path length are retained with the sampler.
