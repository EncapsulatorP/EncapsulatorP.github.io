A tower can reorder a Fourier basis—when it is a permutation

Tower-generated row indices preserve a finite Fourier basis exactly when they visit every remainder once.

Tower / Hadamard recipes · 13–19 November 2025

N ≥ 1 integer, t_j ∈ ℤ, j,k = 0,…,N−1
U_jk = N^(−1/2) exp(2πi t_j k/N)
U is unitary ⇔ t₀,…,t_(N−1) are distinct modulo N
Key formula

Distinct remainders ⇔ orthogonal Fourier rows.

Why follow it

The old recipe becomes a precise design gate: test the arithmetic selector before interpreting a spectral or learning effect.

The inner product of rows j and ℓ is a finite geometric sum. It is zero when t_j−t_ℓ is nonzero modulo N and one when those remainders agree.

With N rows, pairwise distinct remainders give a permutation of the ordinary Fourier basis. A repeated remainder creates duplicate rows and rank loss.

A tower selector can have short cycles modulo N. Orthogonality therefore comes from the verified permutation property, while any benefit of its ordering is a separate experiment.

Next experiment or question
