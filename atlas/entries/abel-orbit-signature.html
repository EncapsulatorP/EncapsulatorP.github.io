A reciprocal orbit leaves an Abel signature

A discounted reciprocal orbit has an exact limiting signature for eventual cycles, and a distinct conditional limit for orbits tending to infinity.

Recovered orbit-signature conversation · 1 May 2026

H_λ(n) = Σ_{k≥0} λ^k / a_k,  0 < λ < 1
A(n) = lim_{λ→1⁻}(1−λ)H_λ(n)
Cycle (1,4,2) → (1 + 1/4 + 1/2)/3 = 7/12
Key formula

The cycle (1,4,2) leaves 7/12.

Why follow it

It recovers a nonstandard-looking construction whose core is a clean conditional theorem about bounded reciprocal sequences.

For an eventually periodic positive integer orbit, the finite initial segment vanishes under normalization and each cycle position receives equal limiting weight. The signature is the mean reciprocal value around the cycle.

A deterministic positive-integer orbit either repeats a state and becomes periodic, or never repeats and eventually leaves every finite set. In the latter case a_k tends to infinity, so its reciprocal sequence and normalized Abel mean tend to zero.

A nontrivial positive Collatz cycle cannot contain 1 or 2, so its entries are at least 3 and its reciprocal mean is at most 1/3. Consequently A(n) = 7/12 exactly when the standard Collatz orbit reaches 1.

This is an exact asymptotic characterization. It does not supply a finite-prefix decision rule or show that A(n) equals 7/12 for every starting value.
