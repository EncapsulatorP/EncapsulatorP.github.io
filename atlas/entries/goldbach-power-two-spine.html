Where twins can meet a power-of-two centre

The prime-pair coordinates reveal an exact congruence restriction along centres m=2^k.

Recovered Goldbach geometry story

m=2^k, k≥1; p=m−d, q=m+d both >3 prime
⇒ d ≡ 3 (mod 6)
The twin line d=1 meets this spine only at 3+5.

Why follow it

It is a small proved obstruction with a clear geometric interpretation.

The centre is even and both primes are odd, so d is odd. The centre is nonzero modulo 3; if d were nonzero modulo 3, one of m−d and m+d would be divisible by 3.

For primes above 3, this forces d≡0 modulo 3, hence d≡3 modulo 6. At d=1 the only admissible multiple of 3 is 3 itself, giving m=4 and the pair 3,5.

The congruence restricts a slice of the prime-pair field. It does not supply a prime pair at every surviving centre.
