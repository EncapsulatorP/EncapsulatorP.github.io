Prime towers made of carries

A multinomial coefficient produces a finite tower of prime valuations. Each height can be computed from either floor sums or digit sums.

Prime-boundary bridge · 30 May and 3 June 2026

A_n = (6n)! / ((3n)! (n!)³),  h_p(n) = v_p(A_n)
h_p(n) = Σ_j(⌊6n/p^j⌋ − ⌊3n/p^j⌋ − 3⌊n/p^j⌋)
= (s_p(3n) + 3s_p(n) − s_p(6n))/(p−1)

Why follow it

It supplies exact arithmetic features for the proposed bridge from wheel classes to Goldbach counts and tower geometry.

A_n is the multinomial coefficient for a partition of 6n into 3n, n, n, n, so it is an integer. Legendre’s factorial formula gives both expressions for its p-adic height.

The digit-sum form identifies the role of carries in base p. The companion compares direct factor extraction, floor sums and digit sums for 708 cases.

Using these heights to explain prime-pair counts is an exploratory application. The exact valuation formula does not by itself establish predictive information beyond existing arithmetic features.
