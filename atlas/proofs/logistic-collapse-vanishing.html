01 · EXACT COMPLEMENTARY-SEED FOLD

Logistic collapse and vanishing terms

For fr(x) = rx(1 − x), with 0 < r ≤ 4 and x in [0,1], complementary seeds merge after one update. The identity itself holds over the reals.

Copy exact formula
f_r(u)-f_r(v)=r(u-v)(1-u-v)
Copy exact formula
x_0=a,\ y_0=1-a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_1=y_1=ra(1-a)

For r ≠ 0, distinct inputs have equal outputs exactly when they sum to one. Applying the same map again preserves equality, so xn = yn for every n ≥ 1.

What disappears?

The gap between this complementary pair disappears. The image of an entire interval is generally still an interval.

Track the mean and the half-gap

Copy exact formula
m=\frac{x+y}{2},\qquad d=\frac{x-y}{2}
Copy exact formula
m'=r\left(m(1-m)-d^2\right),\qquad d'=r(1-2m)d

The complementary line m = 1/2 maps to d′ = 0, with m′ = r(1/4 − d²). Its image remains one-dimensional in general. The full paired map retains local rank at complementary points with d ≠ 0:

Copy exact formula
f_r'(1/2+d)\,f_r'(1/2-d)=-4r^2d^2\ne0

A zero gap on the image of one chosen line does not make the full two-dimensional map singular there.

A different zero: the critical-point derivative

Copy exact formula
f_r(1/2+h)=r/4-rh^2

At x = 1/2 the first derivative is zero, but quadratic variation survives. A flat tangent alone does not collapse a neighborhood.

02 · NEW DERIVATION IN THIS EDITION

The restoring cubic survives

Put r* = 1 + √6 and f = fr*. The nontrivial two-cycle has points p± and derivatives d±:

Copy exact formula
p_\pm=\frac{r_*+1\pm\sqrt{2}}{2r_*},\qquad d_\pm=-1\mp\sqrt{2}

Their product is −1. With a = √2 − 1, the paired operator is C(v₁,v₂) = (a v₂, −v₁/a). Thus C² = −I, C⁴ = I, and det C = 1. Its rank is two; no direction disappears. Rescaling by S = diag(√a,1/√a) makes it a clockwise quarter-turn.

Expand the second return

Let F = f∘f. Since F(p±) = p± and F′(p±) = −1, its exact quartic expansion is

Copy exact formula
F(p_\pm+h)-p_\pm=-h+a_\pm h^2+b_\pm h^3-r_*^3h^4,
Copy exact formula
a_\pm=-r_*(2\pm3\sqrt{2}),\qquad b_\pm=-2r_*^2(1\pm\sqrt{2}).

The coefficients follow from the chain rule; f″ = −2r* and f‴ = 0 give

Copy exact formula
F''(p)=-2r_*\left(f'(p)^2+f'(f(p))\right),
Copy exact formula
F'''(p)=12r_*^2f'(p).

Divide these by 2 and 6 respectively, then substitute d±. The quartic coefficient is −r*³ because f∘f has that leading coefficient.

Compose two returns

For G(h) = −h + ah² + bh³ + O(h⁴), substitution gives G(G(h)) = h − 2(a²+b)h³ + O(h⁴). The two quadratic contributions cancel. Here a±²+b± = 10r*²(2±√2). Therefore

Copy exact formula
f^{\circ4}(p_\pm+h)-(p_\pm+h)=-K_\pm h^3+O(h^4),
Copy exact formula
K_\pm=20r_*^2(2\pm\sqrt{2})>0.

Exactly what vanishes

Define D as the fourth iterate of f minus the identity. At each cycle point p, its value, first derivative and second derivative are zero; its third derivative is −6K ≠ 0. Thus D vanishes to exactly order three. The first derivative of the fourth iterate itself is 1.

03 · LOCAL ATTRACTION WITH ZERO EXPONENT

Local attraction at a zero exponent

Fix either cycle phase p and its positive coefficient K. The fourth-return error map is

Copy exact formula
g(h)=f^{\circ4}(p+h)-p=h-Kh^3+O(h^4).

Choose δ > 0 so that the remainder has magnitude at most (K/2)|h|³ for |h| < δ, and δ² < 2/(3K). Then, for 0 < |h| < δ,

Copy exact formula
0<1-\frac{3K}{2}h^2\leq\frac{g(h)}{h}\leq1-\frac{K}{2}h^2<1.

The error keeps its sign and decreases in magnitude. It remains inside this neighborhood. If its decreasing magnitude approached a positive limit, the upper factor would stay bounded below one, a contradiction. Thus the limit is zero.

Copy exact formula
g^{\circ n}(h_0)\longrightarrow0\qquad(|h_0|<\delta).

Continuity of f and its second and third iterates gives the corresponding limits between fourth returns. The two-cycle is locally attracting at r*: the conclusion holds for sufficiently small initial errors.

The decay is a power law

For nonzero h₀ in this neighborhood, hₙ never becomes zero. Expanding the reciprocal square gives

Copy exact formula
\frac{1}{h_{n+1}^2}-\frac{1}{h_n^2}=2K+O(h_n).

Because hₙ → 0, sum this relation and divide by n. The average error tends to zero, giving

Copy exact formula
\lim_{n\to\infty}n h_n^2=\frac{1}{2K}.

The signed error is asymptotic to sign(h₀)/√(2Kn). Here one n-step means four original logistic updates.

Why a zero Lyapunov exponent fits

Copy exact formula
\lambda=\frac12\log|d_+d_-|=0.

The flat tangent hides a restoring cubic

The linear exponent is zero. Higher-order terms still pull nearby points toward the cycle, at inverse-square-root speed instead of uniform exponential speed.

This derivation was added after the earlier collected-proof review. Exact symbolic simplification checks the quartic coefficients and composition identity; the written inequalities prove local attraction and its rate.

04 · RECOVERED C3.2Z GEOMETRY

A hidden circle or a shrinking circle?

The C3.2Z Hopf detector draws the analytic attracting radius R = √max(0,μ). It distinguishes the state from its detector output. It does not numerically integrate a differential equation.

To make its comparison model explicit, define the normalized Hopf equation with ω ≠ 0:

Copy exact formula
\dot z=(\mu+i\omega)z-|z|^2z,
Copy exact formula
\dot\rho=\mu\rho-\rho^3,\qquad \dot\theta=\omega.

1. A parameter family really shrinks

For μ > 0 the attracting periodic orbit has radius √μ. These circles approach the origin as μ decreases to zero. For μ ≤ 0 the origin attracts; at μ = 0 radial attraction is algebraic. Every positive-radius circle remains one-dimensional. At the limiting parameter the attracting set is a point.

2. A detector can hide a surviving circle

Copy exact formula
z+\overline{z}=2R\cos\theta,\qquad |z|^2=R^2.

At fixed μ > 0, the mirror-sum detector shows a line segment, while power alone is constant. The underlying state still rotates on a circle. A detection floor can also hide a surviving small loop.

State and observation are different objects

Keep the source's separate readings for attractor dimension, visible dimension and visible holes. A projection does not itself change the dynamics.

3. The bubble/tube is a separate toy

Copy exact formula
(R,L)\longmapsto(0.7R,1.15L),\qquad (R_0,L_0)=(1,1).

Exactly R_n = 0.7^n tends to zero while L_n = 1.15^n tends to infinity. Every finite radius is positive. These fixed factors define the website’s transport model; a proposed branch-dependent rule would require a separate update equation.

05 · A DEFINED TOY; BRANE INTERPRETATION OPEN

Define the geometric connection

Working title: vanishing branes

“Vanishing branes” names the proposed research connection. In the supplied mathematical sources, its state space and evolution have not been defined. The product construction below is an explicit candidate that can be checked.

A complete claim needs a state space and metric; an evolving object; a map or flow; the quantity that disappears; and a theorem stating whether disappearance is finite, asymptotic, a parameter limit, or an observational identification.

An explicit candidate: transport plus a shrinking transverse error

Use the p₊ error map g and δ from the local-attraction proof. Define the product model

Copy exact formula
X=S^1\times(-\delta,\delta),
Copy exact formula
\Phi(\theta,h)=\left(\theta+\omega\ {\rm mod}\ 2\pi,\ g(h)\right).

The circle M = S¹ × {0} is invariant. In the product metric,

Copy exact formula
{\rm dist}\left(\Phi^{\circ n}(\theta,h),M\right)=|g^{\circ n}(h)|\longrightarrow0.

The angle follows its prescribed rotation. For nonzero small h, the transverse distance is asymptotic to 1/√(2K₊n). This follows from the scalar proof. The conclusion is asymptotic attraction to the invariant circle within the defined model.

A precise comparison with the Hopf model

At criticality, the radial equation ρ̇ = −Kρ³ has exact time-one map

Copy exact formula
\rho\longmapsto\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1+2K\rho^2}}=\rho-K\rho^3+O(\rho^5).

It shares the leading restoring cubic and inverse-square-root decay scale with g after matching K. That is an established comparison of local leading terms.

A full conjugacy needs an explicit Ψ, a domain and regularity assumptions, plus a proof of Ψ∘Φlogistic = ΦHopf∘Ψ. None is supplied here.

06 · EXACT ASYMPTOTIC ADDENDUM

Three small quantities, three scales

To compare coefficient decay with the dyadic remainder, take odd integers p ≥ 3 and define

Copy exact formula
Z(p)=\exp\left(\frac{\pi\zeta(p-1)}p\right)+1,
Copy exact formula
c_p=\frac{\zeta(p-1)}{p\pi^{p-1}}=\frac{2^{p-2}|B_{p-1}|}{p!}>0.

The rational formula follows from Euler's even-zeta identity. Primality is unnecessary; the following limits also hold along odd primes.

Coefficient decay and value decay

Because ζ(p−1) → 1, and (eᵗ−1)/t → 1 at zero,

Copy exact formula
c_p\sim\frac{1}{p\pi^{p-1}},\qquad Z(p)-2\sim\frac{\pi}{p}.

Indeed cₚπᵖ = πζ(p−1)/p exactly. Exponential coefficient decay becomes a 1/p scale in the final value; the factors cannot be considered independently.

The zeta tail has a dyadic leading term

Copy exact formula
\zeta(s)-1=2^{-s}+\sum_{n\geq3}n^{-s}\qquad(s>1),
Copy exact formula
0<\sum_{n\geq3}n^{-s}\leq3^{-s}\left(1+\frac3{s-1}\right).

Add the first term and the integral from 3 to infinity to bound the tail. After division by 2^(−s), the remainder ratio tends to zero. Thus ζ(p−1) − 1 is asymptotic to 2^(−(p−1)).

Compare the separately defined Mahler remainder

Copy exact formula
R_k=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2^{-j}}\right).

Apply t − t²/2 ≤ 1 − exp(−t) ≤ t term by term and sum the two geometric series:

Copy exact formula
2^{-k}-\frac{4^{-k}}6\leq R_k\leq2^{-k},\qquad R_k\sim2^{-k}.
Copy exact formula
\frac{R_k}{\zeta(k)-1}=\frac{R_k/2^{-k}}{(\zeta(k)-1)/2^{-k}}\longrightarrow1.

For integer k > 1, both normalized factors tend to one. In particular, R_(p−1)/[ζ(p−1) − 1] tends to one along odd p, including odd primes. This is an asymptotic equivalence of sequences. A shared dynamical system or a conjugacy would require another definition and proof.

07 · COMPUTATION, SOURCES AND SCOPE

A tiny number is still a number

The SVG's annotation “float64 0 at p=615” confuses a subnormal value with zero. With binary64 subnormals retained,

Copy exact formula
c_{615}\approx9.14330169325329\times10^{-309}>0.

The value is below the least positive normal binary64 number, about 2.22507 × 10^(−308), and above the least positive subnormal, about 4.94066 × 10^(−324).

Specify the arithmetic before naming a zero

With high-precision evaluation followed by nearest binary64 conversion: c_613 ≈ 9.05352 × 10^(−308) is normal; c_615 ≈ 9.14330 × 10^(−309) and c_645 ≈ 1.06151 × 10^(−323) are nonzero subnormals; c_647 ≈ 1.07221 × 10^(−324) rounds to zero.

The first odd index rounding to zero in this specified conversion is 647. Flush-to-zero mode, premature overflow or a different evaluation formula can produce an earlier zero. Also, 615 is composite, so it does not index a prime-only family.

To evaluate Z(p)−2, form πζ(p−1)/p directly and use an accurate exponential-minus-one routine. Multiplying an underflowed cₚ by an overflowed πᵖ is avoidable. Every exact cₚ in the family is positive.
