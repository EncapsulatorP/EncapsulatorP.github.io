Why an onion, not just a donut?

A fixed η gives a Clifford torus in S³; varying the relative phase φ samples its Hopf circles. Several η values give nested tori after stereographic projection. The two colours share each shell but occupy distinct fibres.

The exact mathematics

z=(cos η e^{it}, sin η e^{i(t+φ)}). Multiplication of both coordinates by e^{is} preserves H(z)=(2z₁ conjugate(z₂), |z₁|²−|z₂|²). Distinct fibres have absolute linking number 1. The minimum chordal distance between two full circles in S³ is sqrt(2−2|⟨u,v⟩|), where u,v are their unit complex representatives. Radius below half the global minimum guarantees disjoint chordal neighbourhoods. Screen bricks are glyphs, not physical solids.

What is arithmetic and what did we choose?

Samples are regenerated from the Collatz rules, not digitized from the screenshot. For each colour we take 12 integer samples per layer, with logarithmically increasing n adjusted to the relevant parity. The branch gap at equal continuous x is log₁₀(6+2·10⁻ˣ). The assignment to torus and fibre coordinates is a designed embedding, not a Collatz theorem. Intermediate folding is a visual interpolation, not an isotopy. Remainder and valuation change phase speed only. No convergence claim.
