Claim boundaryThe reset and counter are defined animation rules in arbitrary units. This is not a physical model, demonstrated monodromy or construction of Riemann sheets. “Bitemporal” preserves the supplied concept’s name.

Watch the overlap build. The last four passes stay on the same torus; the bright particle marks the current position. Compare “Superimposed passes” with “Current pass” at the same state, or use “Catch next reset” to pause with both jump endpoints visible.

Rules, limits & source notes

x = (R + r cos v) cos u; y = r sin v; z = (R + r cos v) sin u. Here R = 10, r = 3.5.

  • Chosen update rules: m′ = growth, w′ = Gm, v′ = w, and u′ = speed. A finite time step approximates these rules. When v reaches π, the counter increments and m,w reset to zero; v is set to −π/2.
  • At v = π the particle reaches the inner rim, whose distance from the symmetry axis is R − r = 6.5. It does not reach the center of the torus hole. The correction replaces the original “hole center” wording.
  • The source contains one particle and one rotating wireframe. Its local mesh rotation of 0.05 radians per time unit is retained. The new colored trails retain the last four successive passes at their actual coordinates; this visual superposition does not add particles or alter the recurrence.
  • Gold marks the inner rim. At each reset, a gold ring marks the sampled threshold crossing and a white ring marks the prescribed landing point. The dashed connector represents the reset assignment, not an integrated path. “Catch next reset” pauses after that assignment; “Original view” hides the added trails and markers.
  • Trail colors distinguish consecutive passes; the particle has a white core for visibility. Physical units such as amu and m/s were removed. Reset clears the trails and restores the initial state. Motion starts paused when the device requests reduced motion. If WebGL is unavailable, the same simulation runs in a 2D isometric projection.
