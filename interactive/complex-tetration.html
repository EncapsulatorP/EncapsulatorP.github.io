Claim boundaryThe basin colors report a finite numerical test of principal-branch iteration. Sheet jumps define a separate stochastic experiment. Neither finite survival nor a small successive difference proves convergence.

Iteration, sheet jumps & source notes

z₀ = c; zₙ₊₁ = exp(zₙ [ln|c| + i(arg c + 2πkₙ)]).

  • The background uses the principal logarithm, kₙ = 0. Pixels tinted by the last state’s angle mark a successive-step difference below 10⁻⁴; dark blue means the run has not escaped or met that tolerance in N updates. Brown records an escape. The image samples bases c; the overlaid paths plot the iterated states z on the same complex scale.
  • Each ensemble orbit gets one perturbed base c + σ(ξ + iη), with independent standard normal ξ and η. At each update kₙ is freshly chosen: 0 with probability 1 − pₖ, or −1 and +1 with probability pₖ/2 each. The sheet index is not accumulated. Amber dots mark sampled nonzero choices.
  • The original safety limits are retained: escape at |z|² > 10⁸, a non-finite value, or an exponential real part > 25. “Survived” means only that none of those events occurred within the chosen N. Escape steps are displayed starting at 1.
  • The real presets c = e−e and c = e1/e, the original complex seed 1.32 + 0.38i, all four parameter ranges, the white principal orbit and blue/red ensemble paths are preserved. The logarithm at c = 0 is left undefined instead of silently replacing zero. Bases with |c| < 10⁻¹² are excluded at the source’s numerical cutoff; survival uses only valid ensemble paths and reports excluded attempts separately.
  • Resample draws a fresh ensemble; resizing preserves it. The basin is drawn progressively at a finite raster resolution so the controls remain responsive. Changing pₖ, σ or M changes the ensemble, while the background remains a principal-branch experiment.

For the mathematical convergence problem behind the Shell–Thron name, see I. N. Baker and P. J. Rippon, Convergence of infinite exponentials (1983). The random sheet choices here are Gemini’s chosen visual experiment.
