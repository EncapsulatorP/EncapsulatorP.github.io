Claim boundaryThese are exact statistics of a chosen binary representation for even totals from 10 through 150. A prime pair makes ρ < ½ for that total; this identity does not establish a pair for every even integer.

Definitions, normalization & source notes

P(k) = 1 if k is prime, and 0 otherwise, for k = 1,…,N−1.
JP(k) = P(N−k); J²P = P.
P±(k) = (P(k) ± P(N−k))/2.
E± = Σk=1N−1 P±(k)²; ρ = E−/(E+ + E−).

The energies use the ordinary sum of squared coordinates, with no division by N. The factor ½ belongs to each projection before squaring. The plot folds the full vector into k = 1,…,N/2: each off-center orbit represents two coordinates, while the fixed point k = N/2 is counted once.

M = ΣP(k) = π(N−1); C = ΣP(k)P(N−k).
E+ + E− = M; E+ − E− = C.
E+ = (M + C)/2; E− = (M − C)/2.
ρ = ½ − C/(2M).

C counts ordered prime representations. If G counts unordered pairs, then C = 2G − D, where D = 1 when N/2 is prime and 0 otherwise. Thus ρ < ½ is equivalent to finding at least one prime pair at the selected N. The ratio depends on this finite, unweighted indicator representation; it is not a new bound or a proof of Goldbach’s conjecture.
